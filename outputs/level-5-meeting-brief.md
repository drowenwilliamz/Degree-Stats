# Full-time Level 5 progression review

Prepared by Andy and Owen for the APR follow-up meeting

Following your request, we have reviewed the available full-time Level 5 module results from 2022/23 to 2025/26 to identify the modules we recommend prioritising.

The proportion of included module results below 40 has increased over the last two years:

| Academic year | Failed / included module results | Failure rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2022/23 | 41 / 229 | 17.9% |
| 2023/24 | 42 / 238 | 17.6% |
| 2024/25 | 77 / 323 | 23.8% |
| 2025/26 | 109 / 380 | 28.7% |

The latest increase is 4.8 percentage points, calculated from the unrounded rates. These figures describe module results; individual students may appear in several modules.

Our first priorities are:

- **Group Design Project:** 24 failures from 63 results (38.1%), compared with 6 from 52 (11.5%) in 2024/25. This is the clearest deterioration among the larger modules. We propose examining project milestones, individual and group assessment components, non-submission, and any changes to delivery or assessment. Earlier milestone feedback may help if late difficulties are confirmed.
- **Stress Analysis & CAE:** 19 failures from 64 results (29.7%), compared with 12 from 54 (22.2%). We propose reviewing calculation and software components, prerequisite understanding, and opportunities for supported practice.
- **Thermodynamics & Combustion:** 16 failures from 60 results (26.7%). Although this improves on 18 from 54 (33.3%) in 2024/25, the volume remains substantial. We propose reviewing topic-level difficulties and formative assessment participation before targeting additional support.

Together, these three modules account for **59 of the 109 failed results (54.1%)** in 2025/26.

**Automotive Chassis Systems** also warrants individual case review: 6 of 7 results are below 40 (85.7%). The small cohort means we should be cautious about drawing wider conclusions. We would also keep Alternative Propulsion Systems (15 failures), Motorsport Dynamics (13), and Motorcycle Dynamics (6 of 12) under review.

We recommend using our meeting to agree the priority modules and the evidence needed to establish their actual contribution to non-progression. We need to link these results to reassessment, compensation and final progression decisions, and distinguish non-submission from assessed failure. This will allow us to identify affected students without counting those with several failed modules more than once.

There are two further qualifications: the source extraction excludes zero overall marks, so genuine zero outcomes may be missing; and the snapshot is dated 1 June 2026, so we need to confirm whether the 2025/26 results are complete and final. Our findings therefore provide a shortlist for investigation rather than a confirmed measure of retention or progression impact.

Once we have checked the assessment and student-level evidence, we can agree targeted interventions, an owner and a review date for each action.

Andy and Owen
