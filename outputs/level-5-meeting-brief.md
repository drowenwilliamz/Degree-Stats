# Full-time Level 5 progression review

Prepared by Andy and Owen for the APR follow-up meeting. Updated 6 November 2026.

Following your request, we have reviewed the available full-time Level 5 results and replaced the 2025/26 summer-board snapshot with the supplied post-resit workbook. The revised data gives **67 failed module results from 359 included marks, a failure rate of 18.7%**.

During this update we found that the original importer had counted worksheet footer statistics, including failure counts, averages and standard deviations, as student marks. These rows have been excluded from the replacement. The previous 2025/26 dashboard figure of 109/380 (28.7%) is therefore superseded. The difference reflects both the updated results and the extraction correction; it must not be presented as a resit recovery rate.

## Proposals for the new validation

The previous Level 5 modules will not run again. We use their results as historical evidence, while our proposals focus on support and monitoring across the newly validated curriculum. We have not assumed one-to-one equivalence between old and new modules.

1. **Early readiness checks and refresher support:** use low-stakes activities to identify gaps in the knowledge and study skills expected at Level 5, then provide accessible support.
2. **Programme-wide assessment planning:** review deadline clustering, communicate expectations clearly and provide formative feedback before major submissions. Use staged checkpoints where appropriate to the new assessment design.
3. **Early engagement monitoring:** identify missed submissions, low participation and attendance concerns promptly, followed by personal-tutor contact and appropriate academic or pastoral support.
4. **Clear reassessment support:** make requirements, feedback, deadlines and available support easy to understand and act on.
5. **Review the first new-validation cohort:** monitor submission, first-attempt outcomes, reassessment recovery and final progression at agreed points. Establish a new baseline and account for changes in assessment structure when interpreting comparisons.

These are preventive proposals for discussion rather than remedies for causes established by the marks. At the meeting we propose agreeing which actions fit the new curriculum, who will lead them and when their effectiveness will be reviewed.

## Completing the progression analysis

We need linked, anonymised first-attempt and post-resit records, compensation/carried-credit information and final progression or withdrawal decisions. This will let us identify affected students once even where they have failed several modules, and separate assessed failure from non-submission.

The existing inclusion rule still excludes zero overall marks and records without a numeric component mark. Genuine zero outcomes may therefore be absent; the figures describe the included results, not a complete official progression rate. We also need the earlier source workbooks to validate the historical comparisons.
