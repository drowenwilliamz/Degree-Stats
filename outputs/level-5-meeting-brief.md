# Full-time Level 5 progression review

Prepared by Andy and Owen for the APR follow-up meeting

Following your request, we have reviewed the available full-time Level 5 module results from 2022/23 to 2025/26 to identify where failures were concentrated and what we can learn for the new validation.

The previous Level 5 modules will not run again. Our recommendations therefore focus on support, assessment planning and monitoring across the newly validated curriculum. The historical module results remain useful evidence, but we are not proposing interventions in discontinued modules or assuming that particular new modules will inherit their outcomes.

The proportion of included module results below 40 has increased over the last two years:

| Academic year | Failed / included module results | Failure rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2022/23 | 41 / 229 | 17.9% |
| 2023/24 | 42 / 238 | 17.6% |
| 2024/25 | 77 / 323 | 23.8% |
| 2025/26 | 109 / 380 | 28.7% |

The latest increase is 4.8 percentage points, calculated from the unrounded rates. These figures describe module results; individual students may appear in several modules.

The largest latest-year failure counts were:

- **Group Design Project:** 24 failures from 63 results (38.1%), compared with 6 from 52 (11.5%) in 2024/25. This is the clearest deterioration among the larger modules.
- **Stress Analysis & CAE:** 19 failures from 64 results (29.7%), compared with 12 from 54 (22.2%).
- **Thermodynamics & Combustion:** 16 failures from 60 results (26.7%). This improves on 18 from 54 (33.3%) in 2024/25, although the volume remains substantial.

Together, these three modules account for **59 of the 109 failed results (54.1%)** in 2025/26.

**Automotive Chassis Systems** had 6 of 7 results below 40 (85.7%). The small cohort means we should be cautious about drawing wider conclusions. Other historical outcomes include Alternative Propulsion Systems (15 failures), Motorsport Dynamics (13), and Motorcycle Dynamics (6 of 12).

For the new validation, we propose:

1. **Early readiness checks and refresher support:** identify gaps in the knowledge and study skills expected at Level 5 through low-stakes diagnostic activities, then provide accessible support.
2. **Programme-wide assessment planning:** review deadline clustering, communicate assessment expectations clearly, and provide formative feedback before major submissions. Use staged checkpoints where appropriate to the new assessment design.
3. **Early engagement monitoring:** identify missed submissions, low participation and attendance concerns promptly, followed by personal-tutor contact and appropriate academic or pastoral support.
4. **Clear reassessment support:** make requirements, feedback, deadlines and available support easy for students to understand and act on.
5. **Review the first new-validation cohort:** monitor submission, first-attempt outcomes, reassessment recovery and final progression at agreed points. Establish a new baseline; changes in module and assessment structures mean direct comparisons with the old curriculum need care.

These are preventive proposals for discussion, not confirmed remedies for causes established by the marks. We recommend using our meeting to agree which actions fit the new curriculum, who will lead them and when we will review their effectiveness.

To complete the historical progression analysis, we still need to link results to reassessment, compensation and final progression decisions, and distinguish non-submission from assessed failure. This will allow us to count affected students once even where they failed several modules. Historical assessment and engagement evidence can inform the new approach without requiring a one-to-one mapping between old and new modules.

There are two further qualifications: the source extraction excludes zero overall marks, so genuine zero outcomes may be missing; and the snapshot is dated 1 June 2026, so we need to confirm whether the 2025/26 results are complete and final. Our findings therefore identify historical areas of concern rather than a confirmed measure of retention or progression impact.

Our aim is to use the previous results to strengthen the transition to the new validation and identify emerging difficulties early in its first delivery.

Andy and Owen
